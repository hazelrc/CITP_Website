Recommendations

Further Activity Needed

To keep the hybrid leak detection system effective and sustainable for the Lama Lama community, several further activities are needed from the client. The system was designed for a remote environment where access, maintenance, and water reliability are ongoing challenges.

Environmental Reliability

One key recommendation is to ensure the system can continue operating during seasonal interruptions. During the wet season, flooding and road closures can make access to key infrastructure difficult for long periods. During the dry season, water demand increases while water availability becomes more limited.

The hybrid design helps address this by combining remote monitoring with periodic manual inspection. This gives the community more than one way to detect leaks and reduces reliance on a single method. Solar power and satellite communication should also be maintained for remote locations to improve reliability.

Community Engagement and Acceptance

Long-term success also depends on how well the local community accepts and uses the system. Some residents may initially be hesitant about new technology being used to monitor water sources. Because of this, the system should be introduced as a support tool, not as a replacement for current local methods.

Rangers and residents should stay involved in inspections, monitoring, and maintenance. Over time, repeated use and training can help build trust, confidence, and community ownership of the system.

Operational Simplicity

The system should remain simple and modular because remote communities may not have easy access to specialist technicians, spare parts, or maintenance support. Components should be repairable locally where possible. More complex repairs should only require outside technicians when necessary.

Training and Staged Implementation

Ongoing Ranger training and community participation should be included in long-term operations. If the system is not used regularly, knowledge may decrease over time, which could reduce effectiveness.

The system should also be implemented in stages. Starting with critical infrastructure first allows the community to gradually adapt to the system, test reliability, and improve performance before expanding to more remote sites.

Further Research

Additional research is recommended to test how well the sensors operate over long periods in remote conditions, including flooding, dust, heat, and extreme weather. Further research should also examine long-term connectivity performance and maintenance needs.

Continuous engagement with Elders, Rangers, and local community leaders is also needed to ensure the system remains aligned with community needs, values, and daily practices.
