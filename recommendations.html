Recommendation and Other Considerations

To keep the hybrid leak detection system effective and sustainable for the Lama Lama community, many other factors need to be considered. The design of the system was created especially for a remote environment where the challenges of access, maintenance, and water reliability are ever-present.

One key consideration is environmental reliability. During wet seasons flooding and road closures can create a situation where access to key infrastructure is impossible for long periods of time. Conversely, during the dry season, increased water demand occurs while water availability decreases significantly. The system must be able to continue operating through seasonal interruptions. The proposed hybrid design combines remote monitoring using sensors along with periodic manual inspection provides two means for leak detection reducing the reliance on a single method for leak detection. Additionally, solar power and satellite communications will provide a more reliable system at remote locations.

Another important factor was how well the local community would respond and accept long-term use. Initially, there may have been some hesitancy from locals about introducing new technology to monitor their current sources of drinking water; however, this technology was designed to support not replace their current methods. Rangers and residents will be closely involved in inspections, monitoring and maintaining the system, with technology primarily acting as a tool to help with detecting leaks sooner. Ultimately, ongoing training and repeated use of this system will build community ownership through increased trust and confidence over time. This goal creates a feasible, community-based solution, rather than relying on outside support.

A second consideration in this project was the operational simplicity of using the technology. Many remote communities do not have easy access to specialist technicians, spare parts or maintenance. Therefore, the designs of the components have been prioritised to be as simple and modular as possible so they can be repaired locally. Any more complex issues that cannot be fixed locally would then require the use of an outside technician only when necessary. This helps reduce the need for ongoing maintenance thus creating greater long-term sustainability.

The project’s success is highly dependent on ongoing community engagement and training. As use of the system becomes less frequent, there is a potential for diminishing knowledge and therefore decreased effectiveness over time. Continued Ranger training and community participation should continue to be included in long-term operations.

In addition to ongoing training and community engagement, the development of the system should be conducted in stages. Beginning with critical infrastructure will allow the community to participate in gradual integration of the system. Testing reliability and improving performance will also allow time for adaptation.

It is suggested that additional research be conducted. Further testing is necessary to improve understanding of how certain sensors are able to operate reliably over extended periods of time in remote locations that experience flooding, dust, and extreme weather. An additional area for research includes long-term connectivity performance and maintenance requirements, as this will enhance future development. Continuous engagement with Elders, Rangers and local community leaders is also critical to the ongoing alignment of the system with community needs, values and daily practices.
