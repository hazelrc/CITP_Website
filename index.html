Lama Lama Leak Detection System

A practical and reliable water management solution designed to reduce water loss and support long-term water resilience in Lama Lama Country.

Project Details

The Lama Lama Country in Port Stewart, Cape York, is a remote community facing significant water reliability challenges (Engineers Without Borders Australia, 2026). Access to the area requires long-distance four-wheel-drive travel, and seasonal flooding during the wet season can isolate the community and delay maintenance or repair activities (EWB Australia, 2026; U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, 2014). During the dry season, water availability becomes more limited while demand increases due to population growth and higher water consumption (EWB Australia, 2026).

This project focuses on Design Area 6: Water Resilience, with the aim of improving water reliability by addressing water loss caused by leakage in the existing water distribution system. According to the EWB design brief, the 80 mm HDPE pipeline connecting the Stewart River intake to the water towers already contains multiple small leaks, reducing the reliability of the community’s water supply (EWB Australia, 2026).

The Lama Lama community requires a practical and reliable water management solution that reduces water loss, supports long-term water resilience, and can be maintained locally despite geographic isolation and seasonal access constraints.

Background

Reliable access to clean drinking water remains a significant challenge for many remote communities in Australia. According to CSIRO (n.d.), over 400 remote communities across the country experience difficulties maintaining a reliable drinking water supply. This issue is particularly significant in Lama Lama, where seasonal environmental conditions place additional pressure on already limited water infrastructure.

During the dry season, the Lama Lama population nearly doubles to approximately 100 people, increasing demand when water supply is already constrained (EWB Australia, 2026). Research also suggests that water consumption in remote Indigenous communities can be significantly higher than urban averages, meaning that even relatively small water losses can substantially reduce water availability (Beal, 2018).

The current water infrastructure in Lama Lama includes an existing pipeline system that already experiences leakage (EWB Australia, 2026). In remote areas such as Port Stewart, leak detection and repair are particularly challenging due to limited access, long travel distances, and seasonal road closures caused by flooding (EWB Australia, 2026; U.S. EPA, 2014). These environmental and logistical barriers can delay maintenance responses and increase the time leaks remain unresolved.

Traditional approaches such as manual inspections and reactive maintenance may not be sufficient in this context. Manual inspections require time, labour, and physical access, which may not always be available. At the same time, highly complex technological systems may not be appropriate because the EWB design brief specifically recommends a “Maintenance First, Minimal Complexity” approach, encouraging solutions that minimise specialist maintenance requirements and rely on systems that can be managed locally (EWB Australia, 2026).

Therefore, there is a clear need for a practical and sustainable solution that improves leak detection, reduces water loss, and supports long-term community-led water management.

Problem Description

The Lama Lama community requires a reliable, low-maintenance, and locally manageable solution to detect and reduce water leakage to improve long-term water reliability.

The current water distribution system experiences leakage, which reduces the amount of water available to the community (EWB Australia, 2026). This issue becomes more serious during the dry season when water demand increases while supply remains limited (EWB Australia, 2026). Due to the remote location of Port Stewart, maintenance and repair activities may be delayed by difficult travel conditions or seasonal flooding (EWB Australia, 2026; U.S. EPA, 2014). As a result, leaks may remain undetected for extended periods, leading to unnecessary water loss and reduced supply reliability. An effective solution should enable earlier leak identification, minimise maintenance complexity, and support local ownership so that the system remains practical and sustainable over time.
