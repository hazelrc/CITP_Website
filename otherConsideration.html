Other Considerations

The success of the hybrid leak detection system depends on factors beyond technical performance. Several non-technical considerations warrant attention during implementation and ongoing operation.

Cultural and Governance Considerations

All project activities must align with the existing Yintjingga Aboriginal Corporation (YAC) governance structure and be endorsed by the Elders Group (Lama Lama, n.d.). Decision-making timelines should accommodate community consensus processes rather than impose external project schedules.

Data generated by the sensor network, including tank levels, flow records, and leak event logs, belongs to the community. Access protocols should be agreed before deployment to respect Indigenous data sovereignty principles (Acharibasam et al., 2024).

Environmental and Operational Constraints

Seasonal access restrictions remain a binding limitation. Although remote monitoring allows leaks to be identified during the wet season, physical repairs may still be delayed by flooded roads (U.S. EPA, 2014).

The system also assumes Starlink connectivity remains reliable. If connectivity degrades during storms, the dashboard will show stale data. Rangers should be trained to recognise this scenario and revert to manual checks for critical infrastructure during outages.

Community Journey Map

Introduction Phase

Months 1–3

Residents may have questions about what the sensors monitor, where data is stored, and whether the system replaces Ranger judgement. These concerns will be addressed through plain-language sessions coordinated by YAC.

Trust-Building Phase

Months 4–12

Early wins, such as the first correctly identified leak, are expected to build confidence, especially when response times visibly improve.

Ownership Phase

Year 2 onwards

Rangers are expected to take full operational control, with the wider community recognising the system as part of normal water management rather than an externally imposed technology.

Some initial scepticism is anticipated, especially from residents who have previously experienced unsuccessful external projects, so transparency about system limitations is essential.
